Birthday was this week. Turned 34. 

No cake reveal. No dinner reservations. No candles. If you’re born in October, you don’t actually get a birthday—you get an entire month where the culture worships fake blood, haunted houses, and 12-foot plastic Home Depot skeletons, and your birthday is just some random Tuesday buried in the middle of it. 

Every porch I walk up to has an inflatable demon or a motion-activated ghoul screaming at me. Satan is trending. I am trying to find an apartment number in the pitch black.

I stream basically every day now. Long, stupid marathons where the stream outlives whatever circadian rhythm I had left. Setting up PaperMC servers, troubleshooting GameCube GX pipelines, testing phone automation scripts, doing late-night deliveries with a mount on the dashboard. 

The deal with going live every single day is that you surrender the right to be treated with dignity. You don't get a special birthday pass. If anything, telling chat it’s your birthday is like throwing chum into shark-infested water. They didn't show up to sing; they showed up to ask why I look like an extra from *The Walking Dead* without needing makeup, or why a guy in his thirties is arguing with Minecraft chunk boundaries at 4 AM.

And honestly, fair enough. You turn a camera on your face for eight hours a day, you’re not an artist—you’re the zoo exhibit. You laugh at the jokes first because if you don't, you look like you're about to snap on camera, and nobody wants to watch a guy genuinely crash out over an emote.

The weirdest part of October isn't chat, though. It’s the delivery runs. 

Delivering food past midnight in suburban Georgia during Halloween month is an accidental horror game. It’s 1:45 AM, pitch black, walking up some winding driveway with a greasy bag of Wendy’s, dodging animatronic scarecrows and fake gravestones that look way too convincing under broken porch lights. You’re out here risking a heart attack on someone’s lawn just to secure a $4 tip on an Uber Pro card so you can cover the gas you just burned and buy an energy drink.

Then you get back in the car, put it in drive, glance over at the secondary mount, and chat is still rolling. Still talking shit. Still demanding to know why the food took twenty minutes.

I could have taken the night off. Shut down the rig, ordered a pizza, closed OBS, and sat in an empty room like a normal civilian. 

I didn't, because sitting in a quiet room on your birthday when your life isn't where you want it to be is way worse than streaming to forty people telling you that your hairline is receding. At least when chat is roasting you, the room isn't quiet. It’s noise. It’s momentum. 

Another year older. Still grinding deliveries past plastic tombstones. Still clicking "Start Streaming." 

Happy birthday to me.
